## K1 — calibration

This is the Amendment 2 K1 predicate: partner-arm e−a in 80–299, oracle
threshold, paired family bootstrap (10,000 draws).

| labels | split | arm | e−a | 95% CI | K1 fires? |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
| v3 primary + nested | id_test | partner | .0740 | [.0182, .1303] | no |
| v3 primary + nested | id_test | nested | .0818 | [.0261, .1370] | no |
| v3 primary + nested | withheld | partner | .0119 | [−.0169, .0412] | no |
| v3 primary + nested | withheld | nested | .0198 | [−.0079, .0475] | no |
| v3 primary (full truth) | withheld | partner | .0119 | [−.0170, .0413] | no |

K1 does not fire because the partner delta is above .01, not because the CI
excludes zero. On id_test the gain is large and its CI excludes zero; on
withheld the point estimate is just above the threshold but the interval still
contains zero. The permitted conclusion is therefore narrow: **the withheld
e−a effect survives the oracle cut numerically, but it is not statistically
distinguishable from zero at this sample size.** It is not a licence to call
the effect robust.

## D2 — H2.1 and H2.2

Condition a, seed 17, raw threshold .5. H2.1 uses the pre-registered raw
long-range false-positive count per sequence; H2.2 compares the fraction of
those false positives lying in a >=4-pair canonical stack with a distance-
matched random canonical null.

| split | labels | H2.1 short mean | H2.1 long mean | H2.1 raw ratio | H2.1 per 1k long-range cells | H2.2 observed | H2.2 null | H2.2 ratio (95% CI) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| id_test | v3 primary | .0611 | 14.3949 | 235.6 | 1.62 | .8100 | .1889 | 4.29 [3.77, 5.11] |
| withheld | v3 primary | .0520 | 7.6178 | 146.5 | 3.89 | .8385 | .2208 | 3.80 [3.45, 4.18] |

H2.2 passes the 2× rule with an interval excluding 1 on both splits.

H2.1 passes the literal raw 2× rule, but its ratio is **not interpretable as a
rate increase**: the denominator is a per-sequence count of long-range FPs, and
a longer sequence has vastly more `|i−j|>128` candidate pairs. The exploratory
candidate-normalised ratios are **1.62 on id_test** and **3.89 on withheld**.
Thus the length trend survives on withheld but not on id_test at the 2×
threshold. A per-100-nt normalization is still large, but the 80–149 nt range
has very few possible long-range cells, so that denominator is also unstable.
H2.1 should be described as split-dependent, not as a clean universal length
effect.

## What remains unproven

- H2.3 (block-boundary false positives) needs P2-block caches; not run.
- D2 is seed 17 only and condition a only.
- No `test_novel` scoring has happened; the embargo is intact.
- The heads were trained on v1 labels, including crossing pairs as positives;
  swapping to v3 at scoring time does not retrain them.
- The oracle threshold is selected on the same data used for the reported F1;
  it is a diagnostic operating-point correction, not a held-out estimate.
