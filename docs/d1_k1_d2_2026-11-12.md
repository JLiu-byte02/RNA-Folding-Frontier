### Region-stratified D1 (template control 1)

Pooled pair-level F1 at the fixed 0.5 cut, production nested decoder, three-seed
mean. Regions are defined by whether each pair's two endpoints lie in the kept
window.

| split | condition | both kept | one kept | neither kept |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| id_test | a | .6650 | .5698 | .5716 |
| id_test | e | .5084 | .0183 | **.0059** |
| withheld | a | .6669 | .5636 | .5452 |
| withheld | e | .5340 | .0237 | **.0090** |

This is the first template control. Under e the model is almost silent outside
the kept window: the one-kept and neither-kept cells are near zero on both
splits. That rules out a template that invents pairs across the rewritten
region. It does not rule out a position/length template operating entirely
inside the kept window; the all-N and shuffle controls test that.

### FP-helix energetics (H2.4 pre-check)

Seed 17, condition a, v3-primary rows. Each long-range FP helix (>=4 contiguous
canonical pairs) was evaluated in isolation with ViennaRNA, and compared with
the RNAfold MFE of the full sequence.

| split | FP helices | FP energy mean | true energy mean | fully in MFE | any pair in MFE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| id_test | 1,136 | −2.44 | −2.32 | 26.6% | 37.5% |
| withheld | 3,807 | −1.78 | −1.23 | 26.7% | 37.3% |

These are not “mostly MFE” structures, but a substantial minority is
energetically competitive and present in the MFE. The PI's pre-registered
expectation therefore stands: if H2.4's Turner-consistent decoder retains these
helices, it can fail for a principled reason; this is not evidence against the
spurious-partner mechanism by itself.

## K1 — calibration

This is the Amendment 2 K1 predicate: partner-arm e−a in 80–299, oracle
threshold, paired family bootstrap (10,000 draws).

| labels | split | arm | e−a | 95% CI | K1 fires? |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
| v3 primary + nested | id_test | partner | −.3110 | [−.3578, −.2647] | no |
| v3 primary + nested | id_test | nested | −.3140 | [−.3610, −.2671] | no |
| v3 primary + nested | withheld | partner | −.2962 | [−.3264, −.2665] | no |
| v3 primary + nested | withheld | nested | −.2977 | [−.3268, −.2681] | no |
| v3 primary (full truth) | withheld | partner | −.2962 | [−.3263, −.2666] | no |

The K1 verdict string now distinguishes the sign. These intervals are entirely
below zero: masking makes the prediction substantially worse, so the premise
“e helps, and the gain might be calibration” is not merely unconfirmed, it is
reversed. H2's P2-central mechanism is falsified as stated.

## D2 — H2.1 and H2.2

Condition a, seed 17, raw threshold .5. H2.1 uses the pre-registered raw
long-range false-positive count per sequence; H2.2 compares the fraction of
those false positives lying in a >=4-pair canonical stack with a distance-
matched random canonical null.

| split | labels | H2.1 short mean | H2.1 long mean | H2.1 raw ratio | H2.1 per 1k long-range cells | H2.2 observed | H2.2 null | H2.2 ratio (95% CI) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| id_test | v3 primary | .0611 | 14.3949 | 235.6 | 1.62 | .8100 | .1889 | 4.29 [3.77, 5.11] |
| withheld | v3 primary | .0520 | 7.6178 | 146.5 | 3.89 | .8385 | .2208 | 3.80 [3.45, 4.18] |

H2.2 passes the 2× rule with an interval excluding 1 on both splits.

H2.1 passes the literal raw 2× rule, but its ratio is **not interpretable as a
rate increase**: the denominator is a per-sequence count of long-range FPs, and
a longer sequence has vastly more `|i−j|>128` candidate pairs. The exploratory
candidate-normalised ratios are **1.62 on id_test** and **3.89 on withheld**.
Thus the length trend survives on withheld but not on id_test at the 2×
threshold. A per-100-nt normalization is still large, but the 80–149 nt range
has very few possible long-range cells, so that denominator is also unstable.
H2.1 should be described as split-dependent, not as a clean universal length
effect.

## What remains unproven

- H2.3 (block-boundary false positives) needs P2-block caches; not run.
- D2 is seed 17 only and condition a only.
- Template controls are now corrected: all-N family-macro F1 is **.00055**
  (id_test) / **.000066** (withheld); dinucleotide shuffle is **.0162** /
  **.0173**. Neither shows a usable template prior. The earlier 0.89/0.90
  shuffle numbers were the `pair_prf` artifact.
- The H2 “e helps” premise is dead: corrected e is about .30 F1 worse than a.
- No `test_novel` scoring has happened; the embargo is intact.
- The heads were trained on v1 labels, including crossing pairs as positives;
  swapping to v3 at scoring time does not retrain them.
- The oracle threshold is selected on the same data used for the reported F1;
  it is a diagnostic operating-point correction, not a held-out estimate.

## v3 cross-check against bpRNA-1m

`scripts/labels_v2_v3_crosscheck.py` matched exact sequences between bpRNA-1m
and Rfam 15.0 seed rows, then compared the two seed projections against the
independent bpRNA label.

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| exact-sequence comparisons | 37,797 |
| unique bpRNA ids | 34,345 |
| unique seed keys | 36,442 |
| mean pair-F1, v2 | .8148 |
| mean pair-F1, v3 | **.9056** |
| comparisons where v3 is better | 5,240 |
| comparisons where v2 is better | 2,156 |

The largest v2 failures are the rows where v2 returned an empty label after a
string-projection parse failure; v3 recovers those structures (examples include
`RF00023`, `RF00003`, `RF00017`, `RF01846`, `RF01959`, all with v3 F1 .83-.99).
There are genuine cases where v2 wins, such as `RF00634`, where v3 proposes a
different register; these are kept in the JSON rather than smoothed away. The
bpRNA labels are not independent of Rfam (they were projected from an older
release), so this is a consistency check, not an external ground truth.
